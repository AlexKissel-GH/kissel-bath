## Where the money goes

Even a "simple" surround replacement involves licensed plumbing work, waterproofing, and finish carpentry. On a typical Budget Shower Renovation, the cost splits roughly into three buckets: skilled labor (the largest share — demo, plumbing, panel setting, and finishing), the wall system and fixtures, and overhead like disposal and protection materials. What you're really buying is waterproofing done right. A shower that leaks into the wall cavity turns a $9,900 project into a $25,000 mold-and-framing job, which is why the cheapest bid on this scope is often the most expensive outcome.

In Denver's older housing stock, one more line item deserves mention: contingency. Galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains in 1950s–70s ranch homes sometimes need attention once the wall opens. We identify the likely conditions at your estimate and put them in the itemized quote, so a discovery becomes a known cost rather than a surprise invoice.

## Where cheap shower jobs cut corners in Colorado

We get called to fix failed budget showers across the metro, and the failures are consistent:

- **Tile over drywall** in the wet zone instead of a proper backer and waterproofing membrane. Grout is not waterproof; Denver's low humidity doesn't save you here.
- **No flood test or valve inspection** before the walls close. A $30 pressure test prevents a torn-out wall.
- **Ignoring hard water realities.** Much of the metro runs hard water that scales polished fixtures and clouds frameless glass within months. On a budget project, we steer clients to brushed finishes and solid-surface walls that shrug off mineral deposits instead of fighting them forever.
- **Skipping the fan.** A new shower pumping steam into a 5×8 bungalow bath without adequate ventilation will peel paint and mildew caulk lines within a year, regardless of how good the surround is.

Solid-surface wall systems earn their keep in this price range: no grout to scrub or reseal, a lifetime warranty on the Sentrel product itself, and an install measured in hours rather than days of tile setting. If you're weighing that against tile, our [Sentrel vs. tile comparison for Colorado](/blog/sentrel-vs-tile-shower-walls-colorado) lays out the trade-offs.

## When the budget scope won't work — and what to do instead

Be honest about your starting point. The budget scope assumes a standard 60"×30" alcove, a functional layout, and a room whose vanity, floor, and toilet still have life. Red flags that push you to a bigger scope:

- A non-standard or neo-angle shower footprint
- Wanting the tub gone entirely and replaced with a dedicated shower (that's a [tub-to-shower conversion](/blog/tub-to-shower-conversion-denver), from $12,900)
- Soft subfloor, recurring drain clogs, or visible water damage beyond the surround
- A layout you fundamentally hate — no surround fixes a toilet that blocks the door swing

Stretching a budget project past its scope is how homeowners end up paying twice. Stepping up to the right scope once is cheaper.

## Where to splurge and where to save

On a sub-$10,000 project, allocation is everything. After 200+ projects across the metro, here's how we advise budget clients to prioritize:

**Spend on:**

- **Waterproofing and the valve.** Everything behind the wall outlasts everything in front of it. A quality pressure-balanced valve and a properly waterproofed wall system are the bones of the shower.
- **The wall system itself.** Sentrel solid-surface panels carry a lifetime warranty, install fast, and never need grout sealing — the best dollar-for-dollar surface in this price range.
- **A quiet, correctly vented fan.** It protects the entire investment from the moisture that undoes cheap remodels.

**Save on:**

- **Designer fixtures.** A solid brushed-nickel set from a mainstream line performs identically to boutique brands at half the price, and hides hard-water spotting better than polished finishes.
- **Frameless glass.** On a budget scope, a well-made semi-frameless door — or even a quality curved rod and curtain — keeps hundreds of dollars in the waterproofing budget where they belong.
- **Accent tile.** Stone-look Sentrel patterns give the custom look without tile labor and grout upkeep.

What never belongs in the "save" column is labor. Unlicensed cut-rate installation is how a $9,900 shower becomes a tear-out. Verify the General Contractor license and insurance before anyone opens your walls.
