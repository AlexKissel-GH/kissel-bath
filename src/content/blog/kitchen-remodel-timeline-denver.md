Most Denver kitchen remodels take somewhere between three days and two weeks of actual on-site construction. That range sounds wide because it is — a countertop swap and a full gut remodel are different animals. But when homeowners ask me how long a kitchen remodel takes, they usually mean the whole journey, from the first estimate to the day they cook dinner in a finished space. That honest answer is four to ten weeks, depending on scope, permits, and material lead times.

## The Full Kitchen Remodel Timeline, Week by Week

For a full remodel, construction is only the middle chapter. Here's how the whole process typically unfolds.

### Weeks 1–2: Design, measurements, and selections

We start with a free in-home estimate. We measure, talk through how you use the kitchen, and give you fixed, itemized pricing — not a vague range that grows later. During this phase you'll pick cabinets, counters, fixtures, and finishes. Homeowners who make selections quickly keep projects moving; those who deliberate for three weeks add three weeks.

### Weeks 2–4: Permits and ordering

If your project involves moving plumbing, adding circuits, or removing walls, we submit permits now. At the same time, we order cabinets and countertop material. Cabinet lead times are the most common bottleneck — stock lines can arrive in days, while semi-custom orders often take three to six weeks. We don't schedule demolition until materials are in hand or confirmed with reliable dates. Nothing wrecks a timeline like tearing out a kitchen and then waiting a month for cabinets.

### Days 1–3: Demolition and rough work

Demo goes fast — usually a day or two. Then plumbers and electricians rough in any relocated lines. If we're removing a wall, the beam and structural work happen here, followed by inspection.

### Days 4–10: Cabinets, counters, and finishes

Cabinet installation takes two to three days. Once base cabinets are set and level, the countertop fabricator templates, and counters go in a few days later. Then come the backsplash, flooring transitions, fixture connections, appliance installation, paint touch-ups, and the punch list. Most of our full remodels wrap on-site work inside 7 to 14 days.

## What Slows Down Kitchen Projects in Denver

After 200-plus projects, I can tell you delays almost always trace back to one of these:

- **Older housing stock surprises.** Denver's 1950s–70s ranch homes — the bread and butter of Aurora, Lakewood, and Littleton remodels — often hide galvanized supply lines, cast-iron drains, undersized wiring, or old vinyl flooring that needs special handling. Homes built before 1978 can also contain lead paint, which is why we maintain EPA Lead-Safe Firm certification (NAT-F212803-1) and follow containment practices on every older home.
- **Material lead times.** Cabinets and quartz slabs drive the schedule. Order early; substitute late and you wait.
- **Permit review queues.** Simple trade permits move quickly. Structural reviews take longer, especially in busy seasons.
- **Change orders mid-project.** Deciding to move the sink after rough-in is the classic two-week delay. Fixed, itemized pricing protects you here — we scope everything before demo so there's nothing to renegotiate mid-stream.

## Permits: Denver CPD vs. Aurora E-Permits

In Denver, kitchen permits run through the city's Community Planning & Development department. Straightforward plumbing and electrical permits are often issued quickly, sometimes over the counter; anything involving a removed wall goes through plan review. In Aurora, the city's e-permits portal handles applications online, and trade permits for like-for-like replacements are usually fast. Centennial, Parker, and Highlands Ranch each have their own building departments with their own queues.

The practical takeaway: permits add days for simple work and a few weeks for structural changes — but only if nobody planned for them. We file as early as the scope allows and schedule demo around approval dates. Our [kitchen remodel permits guide](/blog/kitchen-remodel-permits-denver) walks through which projects need what.

## How to Keep Your Remodel on Schedule

The homeowners who finish on time share a few habits:

1. **Make every selection before demolition.** Cabinet, counter, tile, faucet, hardware — all of it. Indecision is the number-one schedule killer.
2. **Approve long-lead orders immediately.** When we flag a cabinet line with a five-week lead time, the clock starts at approval, not at demo.
3. **Freeze the layout.** Moving a wall or relocating the sink after rough-in means redoing finished work and re-inspection.
4. **Work with one accountable contractor.** When the same company handles demo, cabinets, counters, plumbing, and electrical, there's no finger-pointing between trades when something runs late. That's how we're set up — one fixed quote, one crew, one point of contact.

## Living Through a Kitchen Remodel

You don't need to move out. Set up a temporary kitchen — microwave, toaster oven, coffee maker, and the fridge in the garage or dining room. Paper plates and a dish tub in the bathroom sink get you through the dusty middle week. Water shutoffs are scheduled in hours-long windows, and we tell you the day before. If you have kids or pets, plan to gate off the work zone; doors opening into the kitchen help.

Colorado's dry air is actually your friend here — paint, adhesives, and finishes cure fast. The flip side is that new wood cabinets and hardwood flooring need to acclimate to your home's humidity level before installation, and we build that into the schedule rather than rushing it.

## When a Faster Option Makes More Sense

Sometimes the right answer to "how long will this take?" is "three days." If your layout works and your cabinet boxes are solid, a facelift or [cabinet refacing](/blog/cabinet-refacing-vs-replacement-denver) delivers most of the visual impact of a remodel in under a week, at a fraction of the cost. New counters alone transform a dated kitchen in a day or two. We talk homeowners out of full remodels all the time when a smaller scope achieves what they actually want.
