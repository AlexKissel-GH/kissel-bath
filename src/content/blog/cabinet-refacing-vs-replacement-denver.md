If your kitchen feels dated but the layout works, you're facing the classic question we hear from homeowners all over the Denver metro: reface the cabinets or replace them? The short answer — if your cabinet boxes are structurally sound and you like where everything sits, refacing delivers most of the visual transformation at a lower cost, in 3–5 days, without gutting your kitchen. If the boxes are failing or the layout needs to change, replacement is worth the extra investment. Here's how to tell which camp your kitchen falls into.

## What Cabinet Refacing Actually Is

Refacing keeps your existing cabinet boxes and replaces everything you see and touch:

- **New doors and drawer fronts** in the style and finish you choose
- **New veneer or rigid skins** applied to the exposed box faces so everything matches
- **New hardware** — hinges, pulls, and soft-close mechanisms
- **Finished end panels and trim** for a factory-new look

The result looks like a brand-new kitchen because, visually, it is. The skeleton stays; the skin is new. Our cabinet refacing starts at $10,500 and takes 3–5 days, and because no plumbing or electrical work is involved, no permits are needed.

## What Full Cabinet Replacement Involves

Replacement means tearing the old cabinets out entirely and installing new boxes. That's a bigger job by every measure:

- Demolition and disposal of existing cabinets
- New cabinet boxes, usually stock or semi-custom
- Countertops typically come off and often can't be reused
- Sink, faucet, and sometimes appliance disconnection and reconnection
- Potential drywall, flooring, and paint repair where old cabinets sat

Replacement is normally part of a broader kitchen remodel, which is custom-quoted based on layout and materials. The timeline stretches accordingly, and if you're moving anything mechanical, permits and inspections enter the picture.

## When Refacing Is the Right Call

Choose refacing when most of these are true:

- The cabinet boxes are solid — no sagging, water damage, or crumbling particleboard
- You like your current layout and appliance locations
- The cabinet boxes are standard sizes in reasonable condition
- You want a dramatic visual change without weeks of construction
- Budget matters and you'd rather spend the difference on [countertops](/blog/quartz-vs-granite-countertops-colorado) or appliances

Here's something we see constantly: Denver's 1950s–70s ranch homes and older bungalows were often built with genuinely good cabinets — solid wood boxes, dovetailed drawers, real plywood. Those boxes are frequently better made than budget new cabinets today. Tearing them out is a waste of good material.

## When Replacement Wins

Replace when:

- Boxes show water damage, mold, or structural failure
- The layout fundamentally doesn't work — you want an island, a relocated range, or a wall opened up
- Cabinets were cheap particleboard to begin with and are swelling or delaminating
- You're doing a full remodel anyway and want everything built to current standards

If your gut feeling is that the layout is the real problem, not the look, refacing will leave you disappointed. That's a replacement conversation.

## Colorado Factors: Dry Air and Older Cabinets

Denver's climate deserves a mention here. Our low humidity causes solid wood to contract, and older cabinet doors sometimes show hairline cracks at joints — a cosmetic issue, not a structural one. When refacing, material choice matters: quality veneers and properly finished doors handle our dry air well, and modern adhesives cure reliably at altitude when installed by experienced crews. It's one more reason refacing done right outlasts a cheap DIY attempt.

Hard water is another Front Range reality. Cabinet finishes near sinks take a beating from mineral deposits, so we guide clients toward durable, cleanable finishes in those zones regardless of which path they choose.

## How to Decide: A Simple Framework

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Are the boxes sound?** Open every door and drawer. Look for swelling, soft spots, sagging shelves, and musty smells under the sink.
2. **Does the layout work?** If you've never wished the stove were somewhere else, refacing is likely enough.
3. **What's the real goal?** Fresh look = reface. New footprint = replace.
