## What Drives the Price Up (or Down)

Two bathrooms of identical size can differ by $10,000 or more. Here's why.

### Scope: wet area vs. everything

The wet area — tub, shower, and the waterproofing behind it — is the most technical and failure-prone part of any bathroom. Limiting work to that zone (or even just the wall surrounds) is the single biggest lever you have on cost. A [surrounds-only facelift](/services/bathroom-surrounds-replacement-denver/) starting at $6,500 can transform how a bathroom looks in one day, while a complete remodel starting at $19,700 touches the vanity, flooring, toilet, lighting, and paint too.

### Keeping the plumbing layout vs. moving it

Keeping the toilet, vanity, and drain locations where they are saves real money. Moving a toilet flange or rerouting a drain line through a slab or joists adds plumbing labor, patching, and often a permit and inspection. In Denver's older housing stock, moving plumbing frequently uncovers pipe that needs replacement anyway — more on that below.

### Material selections

A porcelain tile floor costs more than luxury vinyl plank. A custom tiled shower costs more than a composite wall system like Sentrel (which we compare in detail in our article on [Sentrel vs tile shower walls](/blog/sentrel-vs-tile-shower-walls-colorado)). None of these choices is wrong — but you should make them with your eyes open about the labor hours attached.

### Age and condition of the home

This is the big one in the Denver metro, and it deserves its own section.

## The Hidden Costs of Older Denver and Aurora Homes

Denver's housing stock is old. A huge share of bathrooms we work on are in 1900s–1930s bungalows in neighborhoods like Wash Park, Congress Park, and Berkeley, or in 1950s–70s ranch homes across Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, and Littleton. Beautiful houses — with bathrooms that were never designed for modern use.

Common surprises we find once walls open up:

- **Galvanized or cast-iron drain lines** at the end of their service life, corroded nearly shut
- **Water-damaged subfloor and joists** from decades of slow leaks around tubs and toilets
- **Unlevel or undersized framing** that has to be corrected before anything new goes in
- **Outdated electrical** — no GFCI protection, ungrounded outlets, or a single 15-amp circuit feeding the whole bathroom
- **Lead paint** in pre-1978 homes, which legally must be handled by an EPA Lead-Safe Certified Firm (we're certified, NAT-F212803-1)

None of this is a reason to avoid remodeling — it's a reason to budget a contingency of roughly 10–15% on older homes and to hire a contractor who has actually opened walls in your era of house. Colorado's dry climate is somewhat forgiving of old moisture damage compared to humid states, but slow plumbing leaks don't care about humidity, and hard water deposits accelerate valve and fixture wear.

## Permits and Inspection Costs

Depending on scope, your project may need a permit through Denver Community Planning & Development (or your city's building department — Aurora, Lakewood, Centennial, and the others each run their own). Moving plumbing, adding circuits, or changing the structure generally requires one; like-for-like fixture swaps generally don't. We wrote a full breakdown in our guide to [bathroom remodel permits in Denver](/blog/bathroom-remodel-permits-denver).

Permit fees themselves are usually a few hundred dollars — the real cost of unpermitted work shows up later, at resale or when a claim gets denied. As a licensed and insured General Contractor, we pull the permits our projects require and schedule the inspections.

## How to Keep Your Budget Under Control

Practical advice from 200+ projects:

- **Fix the scope before you fix the materials.** Decide "wet area only" vs. "full remodel" first; it changes the budget more than any tile choice.
- **Don't move plumbing unless it buys you real function.** A better layout is worth it; moving a toilet two feet for aesthetics usually isn't.
- **Choose one splurge.** A statement vanity or a frameless glass door, not both on a tight budget.
- **Use composite wall systems in the shower.** Sentrel gives you the look of stone or tile at a fraction of the labor cost, with a lifetime warranty, and it shrugs off Colorado's hard water better than grout.
- **Get a fixed, itemized price in writing.** "Allowances" and vague line items are where budgets go to die.
- **Ask about financing.** We offer financing with a soft credit check, and qualified buyers can get 0% APR for 18 months — often smarter than cutting scope to fit cash on hand.
